COURSE OVERVIEW: 
Geospatial technology today lies at the core of modern decision-making across governance, 
business, environment, and society. With the rapid advancement of technologies such as GeoAI, 
cloud computing, real-time data systems, and digital twins, geospatial systems have evolved as 
an intelligent platform for analysis, prediction, and problem-solving. 
This course introduces students to Geospatial Technology, enabling them to understand how 
location-based data is captured, analyzed, and applied in real-world contexts. It integrates 
Geographic Information Systems (GIS), Remote Sensing, Global Navigation Satellite Systems 
(GNSS), and emerging geospatial technologies within a unified learning framework. 
The curriculum emphasizes conceptual understanding, spatial thinking, and real-world 
applications, supported by hands-on experience, enabling students to work with interactive maps, 
real datasets, and cloud-based tools. 
Aligned with the National Education Policy (NEP 2020), National Geospatial Policy 2022, and 
emerging industry needs, this course aims to develop future-ready learners equipped with 
analytical, technological, and problem-solving skills. Also, it will help students acquire skills for 
further study and for entering the professional world.

CAREER PROGRESSION 
 
The industry has evolved from a linear path to a multi-dimensional career ladder. While the 
foundational progression still begins with operational roles, the new framework integrates 
advanced data science and management tracks to reflect the current industry landscape. 
Today’s vertical mobility allows a professional to transition from data collection to high-level 
strategic oversight through the following stages: 
 
 
Operational Foundation: 
 GIS Operator: The starting point, focusing on fundamental data entry and map creation. 
 GIS Assistant / Technician: Supporting the maintenance of geospatial databases and performing 
routine spatial tasks. 
 
Analytical Transition: 
 Remote Sensing/ Image Analyst / SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) Analyst: Interprets 
information about the Earth’s surface using satellite and aerial imagery. 
 GIS Analyst: Moving beyond data entry to perform complex spatial analysis, querying, and 
layer overlays. 
 Senior GIS Analyst: Leading technical projects and ensuring data accuracy, precision, 
and reliability. 
 
Technological Specialization 
 Professionals can now "branch out" vertically into specialized tracks such as Image 
Scientist or Geospatial Programmer. 
 
Advanced Level (Leadership & Innovation) 
 GIS Scientist / Manager: The visionary tier, overseeing the integration of GeoAI, Digital 
Twins, and Big Data to drive organizational decision-making. 
 Chief Geospatial Officer: Coordinating geospatial workflows across different departments 
and technology.

The two internal examiners, assigned for the conduct and assessment of Practical Examinations

Procedure for Record of Marks in the Practical answer-books: 
 
The examiner will indicate separately marks of practical examination on the title page of the 
answer-books under the following heads:- 
Project - 10 marks 
Students may be assigned projects for the final practical from below. 
Viva based on Project -05 marks 
 
The teacher conducting the final practical examination may ask verbal questions related to the 
project, if any, done by the student. Alternatively, if no project has been assigned to the students, 
viva may be based on questions of practical nature from the field of subject as per the Curriculum 
Practical File - 10 Marks 
Students to prepare a power point presentation / assignment / practical file / report. Instructor 
shall assign them any assignment based on the list of practical. 
Suggested list of Practical - 
1. From the Survey of India Toposheets (educational series) do an exercise of Map 
reading and interpretation with practical understanding of Scale, distance measurement 
and basic elements of cartography/mapmaking. Study and prepare a detail report on it 
covering all the important natural and man-made features, type of land use, nature of 
terrain and economic activities. 
2. Neighborhood Spatial Relations and Pattern Recognition: Observe an area in the 
map and write a report describing the spatial relationship between features (e.g., 
proximity of residential areas to water bodies or commercial hubs). Identify and map a 
specific pattern (e.g., the linear distribution of shops along a main road) and provide a 
spatial reasoning for "why" they are located there. 
3. Satellite Imagery Interpretation: Display satellite imagery on a GIS platform. Identify

five distinct land-cover features and compare how they appear on a satellite image versus 
a traditional vector map. 
4. Spatial Resolution: Download different satellite images from authentic sources like 
Bhuvan platform or UGSS or Copernicus. Categorize and paste them in your file 
according to high, medium and low resolution. Explain the difference in these images. 
5. The Electromagnetic Spectrum and Sensor Bands: Prepare a detailed colored 
diagram of electromagnetic spectrum and mention the important bands for remote 
sensing process. Explain the difference between Panchromatic, Multispectral and RGB 
bands. Download the examples and paste them in your file with explanation. 
6. Data Acquisition to Verification: Use a smartphone or handheld GPS to collect 
coordinates (latitude, longitude, and altitude) of ten points around the school campus. 
Map these points on a digital base map. Write the complete steps and procedure in your 
file. 
7. Vectorization and Attribute Linking: Prepare a Neighbourhood map with the direction 
based route map from school to home or vice-versa. Vectorize the data in point, line and 
polygon. Conduct a local "census" (e.g., types of shops on a street, waste bin location 
etc) and digitize this data into a point or polygon layer as attribute. Do a neighbourhood 
analysis. Explain all the steps with the image in your file. 
8. Environmental Spatial Analysis, using real-time data: Study the weather charts 
prepared by Indian Meteorological Department of India. Prepare a weather report of that 
day after studying all the essential weather elements and explain the relationship 
between atmospheric variables and geographic location. 
9. Vector vs. Raster Lab: Identify and categorize five examples of vector data (points, 
lines, polygons) and five examples of raster data (satellite pixels, elevation grids). 
10. Feature Creation: Using GIS software, create and symbolize a point layer (street lights), 
a line layer (roads), and a polygon layer (parks). Prepare a chart and explain the same. 
11. Understanding GIS Toolbars and Functions: Open a GIS Software and explain it’s 
toolbar. Identify, categorize, and explain the functions of various symbols and tools within 
a GIS software interface. Prepare a table in your file. 
12. Layer Management: Demonstrate the ability to overlay multiple data layers (e.g., 
"Roads" over "Land Use") to visualize geographic information and perform basic map 
creation and analysis.
